How We Worked

Here we explain how we chose our topic, prepared the dataset, divided the work, and worked together throughout the project.

Choosing Our Topic

Tatevik suggested the topic and gave us a starting point for our research. During our meetings, we also discussed other ideas, such as children's library databases and film databases. In the end, we decided to focus on social media and student stress because we wanted to explore how social media habits might be related to stress.

Each of us suggested different problems that the algorithm could investigate. We discussed the suggestions and voted on our final research question. After that, we could start deciding which variables were relevant to our project.

Exploring and Preparing the Dataset

We discussed which variables we needed for our research question. We also considered whether to keep the student identification column and how to deal with missing values and possible duplicate records.

At this stage, we began to divide the main responsibilities. Tatevik focused on cleaning and preparing the dataset, Bibifatimeh worked on the structure of the algorithm, and Mahitab documented the steps we took during the project.

Even though we had different tasks, we continued to work together. We shared ideas, checked each other's work, and helped one another when we ran into problems. We often worked in a shared Zoom room, where we could follow each other's progress, suggest changes, and discuss questions as they came up.

Our Meetings and Communication

We met every day to talk about our progress and decide what to do next. We also used WhatsApp to share updates, ask questions, and discuss problems between meetings. This helped us stay in touch and know what everyone was working on.

How We Made Decisions

We discussed important decisions as a group and listened to each other's opinions. For example, we talked about which variables to use, which columns to keep, and how to handle missing values in the dataset.

When we had different opinions, we talked about the options and tried to agree on what worked best. If needed, we voted. Each person took the lead on tasks related to their responsibilities, but we could all share suggestions and give feedback.

Our Workflow

We worked through the project step by step:

  1. Choosing the topic: We discussed several ideas before choosing social media and student stress.
  2. Finding the dataset: We found a suitable existing dataset on Kaggle.
  3. Formulating the research question: We decided to investigate three social media habits.
  4. Preparing the data: We discussed which variables were useful and how to organize the dataset.
  5. Dividing the work: We assigned the main tasks according to our responsibilities and skills.
  6. Developing the project: We worked on the dataset, algorithm, index, and written documentation.
  7. Reviewing our work: We read through the work, shared suggestions, and made changes together.

Documentation and GitHub

Before uploading content to GitHub, we discussed it during our Zoom meetings and wrote it in a separate Word document. Everyone could contribute changes to the document. Once the content was ready, the person responsible for each page uploaded the final version to GitHub.

What We Learned

During this project, we learned more about data curation, algorithms, and working as a team. Preparing the dataset also made us think carefully about which information we needed and how it should be organized. We learned that it is useful to ask questions, share ideas, and get help from other team members when something is difficult.

We each had our own tasks, but we regularly discussed our progress and helped each other when needed. We learned a lot by working through the different stages of the project ourselves. For us, “learning by doing” and “practice makes perfect” describe this experience well.
