Meet the Team

Who We Are

We are a team of three students from different educational and professional backgrounds working together on the project “The Impact of Social Media on Life.” Our team consists of Bibifatimeh, Tatevik, and Mahitab. We share a common goal: to develop our logical thinking and data management skills by preparing a structured dataset and developing an algorithm to investigate the relationship between social media habits and perceived stress among students.

Our different backgrounds allowed us to share knowledge, learn from one another, and approach challenges from different perspectives.

Our Team Members

  • Bibifatimeh, Mathematics Teacher: Responsible for developing the algorithm and its logical structure.
  • Tatevik, Future Librarian: Proposed the project topic, explored and prepared the dataset, and proofread our work to improve its clarity and accuracy.
  • Mahitab, Librarian: Responsible for creating the index, developing the People page, and documenting our workflow and decision making.

Although we divided the main responsibilities, we regularly shared our progress, discussed challenges, and supported one another.

Choosing Our Topic

Tatevik proposed the topic and shared a starting point for our research. During our meetings, we also considered other ideas, including children's library databases and film databases, before agreeing to focus on social media and student stress. This topic interested us because it allowed us to investigate how different social media habits might be associated with perceived stress.

Our research question was:

Which of three social media habits — daily usage duration, late night usage, or frequency of social comparison — is most strongly associated with perceived stress among students?

This question guided our selection of relevant variables and helped us establish a clear direction for the project.

Exploring and Preparing the Dataset

We chose an existing dataset from Kaggle rather than collecting our own data. We used the CSV format because it allowed us to organize the information into rows and columns and prepare it for analysis. During this stage, we discussed which variables were relevant to our research question, whether to retain the student identification column, and how to handle missing values and possible duplicate records.

The columns selected for our analysis were:

  • Student_ID
  • Perceived_Stress_Score
  • Daily_Usage_Hours
  • Late_Night_Usage
  • Social_Comparison_Frequency

These variables represent perceived stress and the three social media habits in our research question. Selecting relevant information helped us organize the dataset around our research goal.

Dividing Responsibilities

We assigned responsibilities according to our individual skills and experience. Bibifatimeh focused on the algorithm, Tatevik on the dataset, and Mahitab on the index, People page, and workflow documentation.

However, we worked collaboratively rather than independently. We exchanged ideas, reviewed each other's work, and helped one another when questions or difficulties arose.

Our Meetings and Communication

We met daily to discuss progress, explore different options, and decide on the next steps. We also communicated through WhatsApp, where we shared updates, asked questions, and discussed issues between meetings. Regular communication helped us coordinate our tasks, clarify misunderstandings, and maintain a shared understanding of the project.

How We Made Decisions

We discussed important decisions together and considered everyone's opinions. These discussions included selecting relevant dataset variables, deciding which records and columns to retain, and considering how to handle missing values.

When we disagreed, we discussed the alternatives and worked towards an agreement. When necessary, we voted. Team members with relevant expertise took the lead on specific tasks, while everyone had the opportunity to contribute ideas and feedback. Our approach combined individual responsibility with collaborative decision making.

Our Workflow

  1. Choosing the topic: We discussed possible ideas and selected social media and student stress.
  2. Formulating the research question: We identified three social media habits to examine.
  3. Finding the dataset: We selected an existing dataset from Kaggle.
  4. Preparing the data: We discussed relevant variables and the organization and quality of the dataset.
  5. Dividing responsibilities: We assigned tasks according to our skills and experience.
  6. Developing the project: We worked on the dataset, algorithm, index, and documentation.
  7. Reviewing our work: We shared feedback, discussed challenges, and made decisions together.

What We Learned

This project helped us develop our understanding of data curation, algorithm development, teamwork, and problem solving. We learned that preparing a dataset involves making thoughtful decisions about which variables to use and how to organize information. We also learned the importance of communication, mutual support, and recognizing each team member's strengths.

By combining individual responsibilities with daily meetings and continuous communication through WhatsApp, we worked towards our shared goal and developed the project together. Throughout this process, we kept two core approaches in mind: “learning by doing” and, of course, “practice makes perfect.”
