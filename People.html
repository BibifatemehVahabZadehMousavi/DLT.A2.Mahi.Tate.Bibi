How We Worked

This page describes how we chose our topic, prepared our dataset, divided responsibilities, communicated, and collaborated throughout our project.

Choosing Our Topic

Tatevik proposed the topic and shared a starting point for our research. During our meetings, we also considered other ideas, including children's library databases and film databases, before agreeing to focus on social media and student stress. This topic interested us because it allowed us to investigate how different social media habits might be associated with perceived stress.

Each team member suggested different versions of the problem that our algorithm could solve, and we selected the final research question through voting. This question guided our selection of relevant variables and helped us establish a clear direction for the project.

Exploring and Preparing the Dataset

During this stage, we discussed which variables were relevant to our research question, whether to retain the student identification column, and how to handle missing values and possible duplicate records.

It was during this step that individual roles became clearly defined. Tatevik focused on cleaning and refining the dataset, Bibifatimeh dedicated her efforts to formulating the algorithm structure, and Mahitab recorded every step to document our workflow.

However, we worked collaboratively rather than independently. We continuously exchanged ideas, reviewed each other's work, and assisted one another when difficulties arose. Much of our work was conducted in a shared Zoom room, allowing all members to actively monitor progress, offer live suggestions, and make immediate corrections.

Our Meetings and Communication

We met daily to discuss progress, explore different options, and decide on the next steps. We also communicated through WhatsApp, where we shared updates, asked questions, and resolved issues between meetings. Regular communication helped us coordinate our tasks, clarify misunderstandings, and maintain a shared understanding of the project.

How We Made Decisions

We discussed important decisions together and considered everyone's opinions. These discussions included selecting relevant dataset variables, deciding which records and columns to retain, and determining how to handle missing values.

When we disagreed, we discussed the alternatives and worked towards a mutual agreement. When necessary, we voted. Team members with relevant expertise took the lead on specific tasks, while everyone had the opportunity to contribute ideas and feedback. Our approach combined individual responsibility with collaborative decision making.

Our Workflow

Our project developed through the following stages:

  1. Choosing the topic: We discussed possible ideas and selected social media and student stress.
  2. Finding the dataset: We selected an existing dataset from Kaggle.
  3. Formulating the research question: We identified three social media habits to examine.
  4. Preparing the data: We discussed relevant variables and the organization and quality of the dataset.
  5. Dividing responsibilities: We assigned tasks according to our skills and experience.
  6. Developing the project: We worked on the dataset, algorithm, index, and documentation.
  7. Reviewing our work: We shared feedback, discussed challenges, and made decisions together.

Documentation and GitHub

Before any content was shared on GitHub, it was thoroughly discussed on Zoom and documented in a separate Word file. All team members contributed their own changes directly to this document. Once the content was finalized, the person responsible for the specific page uploaded the team's completed work to GitHub.

What We Learned

This project helped us develop our understanding of data curation, algorithm development, teamwork, and problem solving. We learned that preparing a dataset involves making thoughtful decisions about which variables to use and how to organize information. We also learned the importance of communication, mutual support, and recognizing each team member's strengths.

By combining individual responsibilities with daily meetings and continuous communication through WhatsApp, we worked towards our shared goal and developed the project together. Throughout this process, we kept two core approaches in mind: “learning by doing” and, of course, “practice makes perfect.”

Tatevik, Bibifatimeh, and Mahitab
