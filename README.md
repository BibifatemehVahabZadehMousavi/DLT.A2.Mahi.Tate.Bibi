(Bibifatemeh) To what extent do daily social media usage, sleep duration, and sleep quality jointly predict students’ GPA, and which of these factors has the strongest independent association with academic performance?
My argument is as academic performance is influenced by a range of factors, and students’ daily habits are often discussed as possible contributors. In particular, social media usage and sleep are relevant to students’ everyday lives. Spending considerable time on social media may be associated with less time available for studying or sleeping, while sleep duration and sleep quality may be associated with students’ ability to concentrate and learn.
This makes it interesting to investigate whether these commonly discussed factors are reflected in actual data. The selected dataset contains measures of daily social media usage, sleep duration, sleep quality, and academic performance measured by GPA. Therefore, it provides an opportunity to examine the relationships between these variables quantitatively.
By applying multiple linear regression, the study can investigate how each of the three factors is associated with GPA while accounting for the other two factors. The analysis can also evaluate how well the three variables jointly predict academic performance. This approach is appropriate because it connects a familiar real-world question with measurable evidence, allowing common assumptions about students’ habits and academic outcomes to be examined systematically.
However, because the dataset is observational, the analysis can identify associations and predictive relationships rather than establish that these habits directly cause changes in GPA.
