Impact of Social Media

Social media is a part of everyday life, and different platforms may attract different groups of students. Their usage habits and stress levels may differ from one platform to another. We find it interesting to investigate how social media habits are associated with students' stress levels. We are particularly interested in daily usage duration, late-night usage, and social comparison.

Our dataset

The original dataset contains survey information about students' social media habits and well-being. For our analysis, we use the relevant variables to investigate the relationship between social media habits and perceived stress scores.

Field definitions

Field name Type Description
Student_ID String or identifier Unique identifier for each participant.
Perceived_Stress_Score Numeric Participant's perceived stress score.
Daily_Usage_Hours Numeric Daily social media usage duration in hours.
Late_Night_Usage Boolean Indicates whether the participant uses social media late at night.
Social_Comparison_Frequency Categorical How frequently the participant compares themselves with others on social media.

Our research question

Which of three social media habits—daily usage duration, late-night usage, or frequency of social comparison—is most strongly associated with students' perceived stress scores?

Our algorithm

Our algorithm reads the cleaned CSV dataset and prepares the relevant variables for analysis. It converts social comparison frequency into numeric values and late-night usage into 1 or 0. Rows containing missing, invalid, or unrecognized values are skipped.

The algorithm calculates three Pearson correlation coefficients to examine the associations between daily usage duration, late-night usage, and social comparison frequency with perceived stress scores. It compares the absolute values of these coefficients and identifies the habit with the strongest linear association.

The results show associations in the data, not proof that a social media habit causes stress.
